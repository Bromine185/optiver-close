The problem

Sixty seconds ahead, index-relative, in basis points

For each of 200 stocks, at each ten-second bucket of the last ten minutes of the closing auction, predict the stock's 60-second WAP move minus the index's. The metric is mean absolute error, unweighted over rows.

targetᵢ(t) = ( WAPᵢ(t+60)/WAPᵢ(t) − Index(t+60)/Index(t) ) × 10⁴

The subtraction pins the cross-sectional mean near zero by construction — which is why predict-zero is not a straw man. The target is near-symmetric and violently fat-tailed: excess kurtosis 22.6, extremes past ±385 bps against a median absolute value of 6.4. And all 200 stocks on a date share one index leg — the second, less obvious reason a split may never divide a date across folds.

The split is the product

Five folds, sixty held-out dates each, a five-date embargo

Nothing is ever split on rows. The unit of assignment is a date_id, and a date takes all 200 stocks and all 55 buckets with it — closing row-level, cross-sectional, and temporal leakage in one move. Dates 181–480 are scored: 300 validation dates, 62% of the timeline.

train (expanding) embargo · 5 dates validation · 60 dates

Per-fold predict-zero MAE runs 7.13 → 5.82 bps — a spread twenty times larger than any model effect Phase 1 measured. Every comparison is therefore paired within fold. With date_id anonymised, what drives that spread is unknowable, and no claim here pretends otherwise.

The ridge's +0.0628 bps (+0.98%) is small but it is not noise: better in 5 of 5 folds, on 97.7% of the 300 dates, on 80% of the 200 stocks. There is linear signal in the obvious microstructure features — worth about one percent of the floor, on a split whose guarantees are asserted. Whether one percent is a lot was, deliberately, a Phase 2 question.

Null results

The kill list

A log that only contains wins is a marketing document. These were measured, written down, and killed.

  • KILLED — carrying yesterday's target
    Predicting the previous auction's target, same stock and bucket, is 42.6% worse than predicting zero (9.102 vs 6.385 bps). Cross-auction autocorrelation: ρ = 0.027. Shrinking it optimally keeps 2.25% of the signal and buys +0.002 bps. Any feature whose thesis is "yesterday's move persists" dies here.
  • NULL — the median
    The best constant predictor beats zero by +0.00019 bps. That is the entire value of knowing the target's location.
  • NULL — imbalance, linearly
    The first thing anyone reaches for in an auction dataset carries a stable but tiny coefficient — 1/28th of the leading feature. Not linear, on its own. (Phase 2 has a rebuttal.)
  • NULL — the bid-ask spread, linearly
    Flips sign across folds; averages +0.022. Three of fourteen features are not measurably doing anything, and saying so is cheaper than implying they are.
