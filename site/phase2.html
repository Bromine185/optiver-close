05Features with memory

Causality is tested by truncation, not asserted.

A Phase 2 row may use past buckets of its own auction, the current cross-section, and previous dates' revealed targets — exactly the live API's information set, nothing more. Every feature must survive three attacks:

Cut today at bucket s

Truncate the frame at bucket s of the last date and rebuild: every surviving row must come back bit-identical. A feature that peeks even one bucket ahead fails here.

tests/test_features2.py · truncation at the bucket axis

Cut the history at date d

Same guarantee on the date axis: rebuild from a shorter history and every surviving row is unchanged. Rolling windows, cross-sectional ranks, and state features all pass.

tests/test_features2.py · truncation at the date axis

Perturb what truncation can't see

Truncation cannot catch same-day leakage through the target. So: scale date d's targets — later dates' state features must move, and date d's own must not.

tests/test_features2.py · the perturbation test

The 5-date embargo Phase 1 carried "for nothing" is now load-bearing: rolling and cross-sectional features are exactly what it was pre-paid for. Because the harness never changed, Phase 2's numbers remain directly comparable with Phase 1's — that comparability was bought in advance.

06What the trees found

Importance is not value.

Phase 1 left a question open on purpose: a null result under ridge is a fact about the pair, not the feature. Answered:

Revived

size_imbalance — from noise to first place

Sign-unstable, ≈0 under ridge. Under trees it is the single largest feature, 24.2% of total gain. Bounded in [−1, 1], no winsorisation, no scale issues — the signal was always there, it just is not linear. The cleanest single demonstration of what the model-class change bought.

…but gain importance still lies

The state family shows 4.3% of gain importance and removing it costs −0.0002 bps — literally nothing. Importance measures where the trees spent splits, not what the splits were worth. Only the drop-one-family ablation below measures value.

The marginal costs do not sum to the total (0.0245 vs 0.0284): the families are partially redundant, and the redundancy is itself information — cross-sectional context can be partly reconstructed from row features plus rolling state.

07Null results

The kill list, second edition.

Phase 1 killed yesterday's target as a level (42.6% worse than zero, ρ = 0.027). Phase 2 finishes the epitaph.

Killed

The revealed-target stream, entirely

Removing the state family (stock_vol_20d, revealed_abs, their indicators) leaves the model exactly equal — −0.0002 bps, per-fold noise on both sides of zero. The volatility state the revealed targets carry is evidently already spanned by the live book: spread, depth, and the rolling realised vol of the auction under way.

Killed for Phase 3: any feature whose only input is the revealed-target stream. Yesterday's target was worth nothing as a level; its scale is worth nothing on top of the live book either.

Recorded, not resolved

The cross-sectional decay

24.4% of importance, +0.0063 of marginal value — and a per-fold profile that decays 0.0134 → 0.0013 across the timeline. With date_id anonymised the decay cannot be attributed: more training data teaching the trees to reconstruct the cross-section from row features is the boring candidate; a regime change is the untestable one. Written down, left open.

08Reproduce it

Two commands, one committed report.

python scripts/run_phase2.py --preset FULL

# + the drop-one-family ablations (1,006 s)   -> reports/phase2_lgbm.json
